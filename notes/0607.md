- from claude about how schism / conversion works rn:

```
The two stages

  Schism is a two-step process each tick, and they live in different files:

  1. Religion::should_schism (religion.rs:58) — does this religion crack this generation? (a yes/no coin flip)
  2. Adherent::try_conversion (adherent.rs:81) — given a crack happened, which individuals walk out into the new sect? (a per-person coin
  flip)

  The orchestration is in tick.rs:32-81: loop every religion → collect the ones that schism → for each, make a new Religion and offer every
  member a conversion.

  ---
  Stage 1: should_schism — does it crack?

                   ┌─ gate ─────────────────────────────┐
                   │ congregation < min_congregation(50)?│ → NO, never schism
                   └────────────────────────────────────┘
                                    │ pass
                                    ▼
     chance =  schism_base_rate  ×  mean_heterodoxy  ×  (1 + high_het_share)  ×  population_factor
               └─────┬────────┘     └──────┬───────┘     └────────┬────────┘     └───────┬───────┘
               your knob (0.01→0.1)   ~0.25, global avg     ~1.0 (≈0 heretics)      size/1000, capped 1
                                      of EVERYONE             doubles at most
                                      (not this religion!)

     then: flip_weighted_coin(chance)   // per religion, per tick

  What each variable means, visually:

  - schism_base_rate — the master volume knob. Everything else multiplies it down, so it's the only big lever.
  - mean_heterodoxy — average "questioning-ness" across the whole population, not this congregation. Starts at 0.25 and stays ~there (more on
  why below). This alone cuts your base rate to a quarter.
  - high_heterodoxy_share — fraction of this religion's members above 0.7 heterodoxy. With a population centered at 0.25, almost nobody is up
  there, so this term is ≈ (1 + 0.00) = basically 1. It can at most double the rate.
  - population_factor = min(size / population_factor_pivot, 1.0). A ramp: a religion of 500 contributes 0.5, a religion of 1000+ contributes
  1.0. Big religions schism more.

  So with your old 0.01, root religion of 500:
  chance = 0.01 × 0.25 × 1.0 × 0.5 ≈ 0.00125   (0.125% per tick)
  Over 150 generations: 1 − (1−0.00125)^150 ≈ 17% chance of ever cracking. That's why you saw basically nothing.

  With your new 0.1:
  chance = 0.1 × 0.25 × 1.0 × 0.5 ≈ 0.0125     (1.25% per tick, → 2.5% as pop grows past 1000)
  Over 150 generations: ≈ 85%+. And the root stays huge, so it keeps re-rolling every tick with no cooldown — it can spawn sect after sect.
  That's your "tons of child religions."

  ---
  Stage 2: try_conversion — who actually leaves?

  When a crack happens, tick.rs:72 offers every member of the parent religion a conversion:

  // adherent.rs:75
  probability = heterodoxy × conversion_base_rate   // 0.02
  flip_weighted_coin(probability)

    member het = 0.25  →  0.25 × 0.02 = 0.005   (0.5% chance to leave)
    member het = 0.70  →  0.70 × 0.02 = 0.014   (1.4% chance to leave)

  Out of 500 members averaging 0.25 heterodoxy, the expected number who convert is:
  500 × (0.25 × 0.02) ≈ 2–3 people

  This is the real story of your model. A schism fires, but the new sect is born with ~2–3 people — far below min_congregation = 50. So:

  - The new sect can never schism again (gated out).
  - It usually dies within a generation or two (everyone ages/dies, tick.rs:44 marks it extinct).

  So what you're getting with 0.1 isn't a branching tree of growing faiths — it's a big stable root continuously shedding tiny stillborn
  splinters.

  ---
  Why mean_heterodoxy stays ~0.25 (important context)

  Two dynamics that would move it are off:

  - update_heterodoxy is commented out (adherent.rs:97-106) — nobody's beliefs drift during their life.
  - Births regress toward the population mean (probability/adherent.rs:36, 0.6×parent + 0.4×pop_mean), which holds the mean near 0.25 rather
  than letting heretical lineages compound.

  So heterodoxy is effectively frozen near the starting mean. That's fine, just know that mean_heterodoxy and high_het_share are
  near-constant — schism_base_rate and population_factor are doing all the real work.

  ---
  What's "realistic"?

  The honest answer: 0.1 looks productive but it's producing junk sects, and 0.01 looks dead. The bottleneck isn't the schism rate — it's the
  conversion rate. Real schisms peel off a meaningful bloc (think 10–40% of a congregation, often the heterodox wing), which gives the new
  faith enough mass to survive and itself schism later.

  If you want realistic, self-sustaining religious trees, I'd suggest tuning the conversion side, not just the base rate. A few options:

  1. Raise conversion_base_rate a lot (e.g. 0.5–0.9) so heterodox members reliably defect. With 0.7, a 0.7-heterodoxy person leaves ~49% of
  the time → a real bloc walks out.
  2. Make conversion depend on the threshold, not a flat scale — e.g. everyone above high_heterodoxy_threshold converts with high
  probability, everyone below stays. That makes a schism actually split the congregation along the belief fault line, which is what
  high_heterodoxy_share is already measuring.
  3. Keep schism_base_rate modest (0.02–0.05) once conversion is fixed, so cracks are occasional but consequential.
  4. Consider lowering min_congregation or letting a sect schism once it grows — otherwise even a healthy 40-person breakaway is permanently
  sterile.
```
